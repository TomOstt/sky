# Top 50 ideas and top 10 breakthrough bets

This file ranks the ideas of the whole report by practical return, and then separates out the ten bets that are hard and uncertain but could change what Sky is. It adds no evidence of its own: every expected gain is copied from the chapter or research note cited next to it, with that source's label (measured, modeled, target or assumption). The scores are judgments made from that evidence. They are not measurements, and they should be re-scored at every gate review ([Part XXVIII](part28-risks.md), XXVIII-T1).

Time horizons use the gates of the spine as [Part XXV](part25-roadmap-12-months.md) §10 places each task: G0 ground truth (weeks 0–2), G1 Metal foundation (1–5), G2 TIR and the oracle (1–6), G3 first tokens (5–9), G4 parity on the M2 Pro (9–14), G5 lead on the M5 Max (14–20), G6 exact speculation and TTFT (20–28), G7 breadth (20–40), G8 proof (30–44). Work beyond year 1 uses the horizons of [Part XXVI](part26-roadmap-4-years.md)'s bet register (§3.1).

## How the ranking works

Each idea gets three scores from 1 to 5.

| Score | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Impact (I)** | no measurable gain | ≤ 3%, or a niche or later workload | 1.03–1.1×, a secondary metric, or the enabler of an impact-4/5 item | ≥ 1.1× on a headline metric, or carries one of the measured openings (long context, small models, M1–M4 prefill, cold TTFT) | a precondition for every claim, or ≥ 1.3× on a headline metric |
| **Confidence (C)** | the evidence is against it (the report rejects it) | depends on unmeasured M5 behavior, on training, or on third-party claims | modeled or EXPERIMENTAL, with supporting data | strong evidence; the outcome is LIKELY | measured on our hardware, or a verified mechanism with no open question |
| **Difficulty (D)** | days | one to two weeks | three to six weeks | about a quarter, several crates | research, or needs hardware or compute the project does not have |

**ROI = I × C ÷ D.** Rows are sorted by ROI, highest first. Ties go to the earlier gate, then to the higher impact. Ideas the report rejects are ranked last whatever their score, because the plan will not build them; they are marked **rejected** and kept so that readers can see why. The ten breakthrough bets of Part 2 also appear in the table, marked Bet A to Bet J, with their honest (low) scores. Part XXVI numbers its own register B1–B11; where a bet appears there, the entry gives that ID.

Short forms used in the table:
- "NA" means the M5 GPU's neural accelerators, reached through Metal 4 MPP `matmul2d`.
- β is the extra verify cost per drafted token; τ is the number of tokens emitted per speculation round; c is the draft cost relative to one target step (R8 §2.1).
- Task IDs such as XVI-T1 are the chapters' implementation tasks. [issues.md](issues.md) turns the first thirty pieces of work into GitHub issues.

## What the ranking says

- The cheapest high-value work is measurement and correctness: the M5 Max day-one kit, the competitor watch, the harness, the oracle and the exactness tests. Several of these decide kill criteria within days.
- Next come the unglamorous pieces that carry the measured openings: the batch-invariant verify GEMV, GQA attention, GPU sampling, the static plan, the aligned relayout cache and MPP prefill on M1–M4.
- Speculation has the highest impact and only medium confidence: it depends on drafters, workloads and the M5's neural accelerators, none of which we have measured.
- The moonshots score low on ROI, as they should. They are where a large gain could come from, which is why Part 2 gives each one a first experiment and a kill criterion.
- The rejected rows at the bottom carry evidence that saves months: megakernels, 2-bit codebooks and command replay do not pay on Apple GPUs.

## Part 2. The top 10 breakthrough bets

None of these is on the critical path to G6. Each could make Sky dramatically better if it works, and each has a cheap first experiment and a kill criterion, so a bet can be dropped before it consumes a quarter. They are ordered by how much they would change the product, not by ROI. Part XXVI's register adds two bets outside year 1's scope, MoE expert streaming (B4) and multi-device (B5), and notes that only the trained drafter, multi-device and ASTC can move the headline numbers by more than about 20% ([Part XXVI](part26-roadmap-4-years.md) §3.1).

### Bet A. Exact speculation as a product property

- **Why it is transformative.** Speculative output byte-identical to Sky's own plain output at any temperature turns a speed technique into a property that can be tested byte for byte in CI, audited, and relied on by evaluation work. mlx-lm cannot claim it: its output departs from plain greedy at token 91 on Qwen3-32B with a 0.6B draft on an M5 Max, on an exact logit tie (#1470), and the maintainers answered with documentation (#1592). It is the longest-lived candidate moat in the report, at 2–6 months for Apple to reproduce, because one reduction order must run through every kernel on the token path ([Part XXVII](part27-moat.md) §3.2).
- **What must be true.**
  - Batch invariance costs ≤ 3% at M = 1 on the large Qwen3-32B shapes and ≤ 5% end to end on 8B decode ([Part XXVIII](part28-risks.md) R-11). On NVIDIA serving stacks it cost 1.62× in vLLM and 24–55% in SGLang (R8 §3.3); batch 1 on Apple GPUs is untested.
  - The verify family stays ≤ 1.3× of M = 1 up to M = 8 (SPINE §3). R3's 60-line test kernel reached ×1.30 at M = 3 but spilled registers from M = 4 (R3 §7.G). Part XXI adds a fallback: ≤ 1.15× at M = 4 on every chip (§2.3).
  - Every kernel on the token path (GEMV, attention, norms, sampling) carries one `reduction_schedule`, which the verifier checks ([Part XXI](part21-speculative.md) §3.1; [Part VII](part07-tensor-ir.md) VII-T12).
  - MLX does not ship cheap verification and exactness first (R-7).
- **First experiment.** XXI-T1 with XI-T11 on the M2 Pro: the verify GEMV for M = 2…8, with bitwise row equality against the M = 1 kernel on random and real Qwen3 weights, its cost curve on 32B shapes, and its cost at M = 1 against a specialized GEMV ([issues.md](issues.md) I-07).
- **Kill criterion.**
  - G1: invariance cost at M = 1 above 3% → redesign.
  - G4: end-to-end cost above 5% on 8B decode → exactness becomes opt-in, and "byte-identical at full speed" leaves the pitch.
  - G6: MLX ships both verification within 1.3× of M = 1 up to M = 8 and exact output before Sky → exactness stops being the headline (R-7, R-11).
- **Chapters.** [Part XXI](part21-speculative.md) §3, §6, §9; [Part XI](part11-metal-backend.md) §9.2, §9.6; [Part XXVII](part27-moat.md) §3.2 and P1; [Part XXVIII](part28-risks.md) R-7, R-11.

### Bet B. A trained DFlash-class drafter for Qwen3-32B (Part XXVI B1)

- **Why it is transformative.** Public drafters set the ceiling. With EAGLE-3 (τ 2.29–3.08 at T = 0.6) the modeled band is 47–59 tok/s, and a 60+ tok/s *average* needs τ ≥ 3.5–4.5 (SPINE §3; R8 §2.9). At τ = 4.5 the model gives 80.8–93.3 tok/s at β = 0.05–0.02 ([Part IV](part04-hardware-limits.md) §8), and R8 §9 puts block drafters at 2.5–3× if a 16-token verify is nearly free. Published drafters of this class reach τ = 5.88 (DFlash on Qwen3-8B, T = 0) and 4.42–5.74 (DSpark on Qwen3.8-27B) (R8 §2.3). It is also the one asset whose cost is training compute rather than engineering time, and the one an open-core release could keep ([Part XXVII](part27-moat.md) §3.9, §5.7).
- **What must be true.**
  - A 16-token verify is nearly free on the neural accelerators (Bet C; R-4 passed at G5). DFlash's drafter is 5 layers plus the full LM head, 0.6–1.3 B parameters streamed per pass, so it needs a large free verify width (R8 §2.6).
  - A pilot reproduces the published 8B training recipe, and τ ≥ 4.0 at T = 0.6 on the declared workload mix is measured before any runtime work (XXI-T12; Part XXVI B1).
  - Compute and people exist: the ML inference engineer and rented data-center GPUs, with a budget unknown until the pilot (Part XXVI §3.1). The tooling is open: Red Hat `speculators` and SGLang SpecForge (R8 §2.9).
- **First experiment.** The 8B half of XXI-T12, conditional at G7 in Part XXV: the DFlash runtime for Qwen3-8B with the public drafter, checking τ within 5% of the model card and ≥ 2.5× plain on HumanEval-style prompts on the M5 Max. Only then the training pilot.
- **Kill criterion.** R-4 failed at G5 (no NA-wide verify), or τ < 4.0 after the pilot (Part XXVI B1). If the 8B runtime misses 2.5× with a public drafter, no 32B drafter is trained.
- **Chapters.** [Part XXI](part21-speculative.md) §4, §12; [Part IV](part04-hardware-limits.md) §8; [Part XXVI](part26-roadmap-4-years.md) §3.1; [Part XXVII](part27-moat.md) §3.9; [Part XXVIII](part28-risks.md) R-4, R-12.

### Bet C. Neural-accelerator-wide tree verification, with decode on the same kernel family

- **Why it is transformative.** The free verify width is 33–35 tokens on the M5's matrix peak, against 8.4–9.0 on shader ALUs ([Part IV](part04-hardware-limits.md) §8). That is the spine's thesis: speculation turns the M5's idle matrix compute into tokens. Realistic trees are 16–24 nodes after tile efficiency ([Part XXI](part21-speculative.md) §5), and R8 §9 models up to about 2.5× greedy with trees if verification holds.
- **What must be true.**
  - `matmul2d` is efficient at 16–32-row tiles. Apple's sample 64×32 tile reaches about half of peak, and tiles of 64×64 or more are needed (R6 §A2). MLX sends most 13 ≤ M ≤ 32 projections to non-accelerator split-K (R3-kernels §d).
  - Verify at M = 16 costs ≤ 1.3× of the M = 1 GEMV (XXI-T2).
  - For byte-identity across tiers, decode at M = 1 must run on the same MPP family and still stream ≥ 95% of the raw read. Otherwise byte-identity holds only for ALU-width verification, and wide trees run in a labeled distribution-exact mode ([Part XXI](part21-speculative.md) §3.4; [Part IX](part09-scheduler.md) IX-T8).
  - `verify_mpp` passes the bitwise row-invariance test on each chip (XI-T12), despite MPP's opaque lowering (R-10).
- **First experiment.** On the M5 Max: MPP GEMM with 8-, 16- and 32-row tiles at M = 1…32 on Qwen3-32B shapes, measuring streaming bandwidth and bitwise row invariance, against the ALU GEMV at M = 1 and the verify GEMV at M = 2…16 ([Part XI](part11-metal-backend.md) §15, item 1; [issues.md](issues.md) I-04, I-23).
- **Kill criterion.** G5, or G1 if the hardware arrives early: if the best `matmul2d` verify at M = 16 is not faster than the ALU verify at M = 16, plan verify widths of about 8 everywhere, drop 16–32-node trees and DFlash-class drafters, and remove "idle matrix compute" from the pitch (R-4). Trees are also disabled on any chip where they do not beat the best chain by ≥ 10% (XXI-T10).
- **Chapters.** [Part XXI](part21-speculative.md) §2.2, §3.3–§3.4, §5; [Part XI](part11-metal-backend.md) §9.4, §9.6; [Part IX](part09-scheduler.md) §8; [Part XXVIII](part28-risks.md) R-4, R-10.

### Bet D. Block-level e-graph search with measured costs (Part XXVI B9)

- **Why it is transformative.** Where rewrites compete, a fixed pipeline must guess: with mixed formats, Q+K together and V alone or Q alone and K+V together; which norm absorbs a scale; where a permutation is cheapest. Saturating one 32-operation layer template, extracting with a DAG-aware exact method priced by Part IX's roofline cost, and replicating the result over all 64 layers could find plans that hand-written rules miss. Tensat found graphs up to 16% faster than the state of the art (R7 §6; [Part VIII](part08-graph-optimizer.md) §5).
- **What must be true.**
  - Competing rewrites actually occur on real files, for example mixed-format GGUF layers.
  - Part IX's cost model ranks candidates correctly; the closure error is ≤ 5% (XXIII-T4).
  - Extraction prices a shared subterm once. Today's `sky-egraph` counts "a subterm used twice … twice" (R7 §6.4).
  - The prior is modest, and Part XXVI calls it the weakest evidence in its register: Cranelift's production e-graph gained about 0.1% (R7 §6), and the decode graph's useful rewrites are a handful of known identities already in Part VIII §3.
- **First experiment.** VIII-T11: `TirLang` in `sky-egraph` with the R7 §6.5 rule set, on the Qwen3 and Q4_K_M layer templates. It must first reproduce the deterministic pipeline's decisions in under one second; then VIII-M5 measures the best extracted plan against the pipeline's.
- **Kill criterion.** No e-graph plan measurably faster on ≥ 2 model families (mixed formats, Q4_K_M layers), or R-13 freezing the general layers at G5 because search improved hot kernels by < 5% over hand-picked defaults (Part XXVI B9). The e-graph then stays off, which is already its default.
- **Chapters.** [Part VIII](part08-graph-optimizer.md) §5; [Part X](part10-autotuner.md) §2.2, §12; [Part XXVI](part26-roadmap-4-years.md) §3.1; [Part XXVIII](part28-risks.md) R-13.

### Bet E. Compiler-chosen mixed bits under a KL budget (Part XXVI B2)

- **Why it is transformative.** Decode speed is bytes per token. Moving 4.5 → 3.8–4.1 average bpw at Q4_K_M-like KL is worth 1.10–1.18× decode (R8 §4.4), more than the plain-decode headroom on the M5 Max. As a compiler pass with a certificate (ε achieved, calibration hash, bpw, bytes per token), it fits Sky's contracts and `sky explain`.
- **What must be true.**
  - Quality holds under a measured end-to-end KL budget. Qwen3 is sensitive: AWQ W3 g128 drops MMLU from 81.2 to 54.9, so 3-bit averages need QAT ([Part XII](part12-quantization.md) §8).
  - Users accept a labeled "different weights" mode. It is never used in a same-file comparison (D5); the fair comparison is mlx-lm's `dynamic_quant` and DWQ at equal bpw.
  - The search is affordable: it needs the BF16 weights (64 GB for Qwen3-32B) and many evaluations, overnight to days for 32B on one M5 Max [LIKELY].
  - Mixed files stream at full speed: at most 15 `mlx_affine<b, g>` codec pairs per file.
- **First experiment.** XII-T12 on Qwen3-1.7B and 8B: at `dynamic_quant`'s bpw, Sky's KLD must be ≤ `dynamic_quant`'s, or Sky must need fewer bytes at equal KLD.
- **Kill criterion.** No win over `dynamic_quant` and DWQ at equal bits per weight (Part XXVI B2).
- **Chapters.** [Part XII](part12-quantization.md) §7.4, §8; [Part XXVI](part26-roadmap-4-years.md) §3.1; R8 §4.4.

### Bet F. ASTC texture-compressed weights (Part XXVI B7)

- **Why it is transformative.** It is the only route found to fewer bytes per weight that spends no ALU on decoding ([Part XII](part12-quantization.md) §11). Qwen3-32B at 3.56 bpw would stream 14.2 GB per token: a 43 tok/s roofline at 614 GB/s against 34.1, so ≤ 1.26×. Apple's 2025 server foundation model stores its weights this way, with LoRA recovery keeping MMLU at 79.2 against 80.0 (R8 §4.2).
- **What must be true.**
  - Texture reads of ASTC 6×6 HDR data sustain ≥ 90% of the bandwidth a buffer GEMV gets, counted in compressed bytes per second. Unknown: the texture units may decode slower than DRAM delivers.
  - Weight matrices tile across 2D textures within the maximum texture dimension.
  - Quality is recoverable on Qwen3, which is more quantization-sensitive than Llama 3. It changes outputs, so it is a labeled mode with a quality budget.
- **First experiment.** XII-T14, as a spike in weeks 44–52 (Part XXVI §3.1): a texture-GEMV microbenchmark (`crates/sky-metal/benches/astc_gemv.rs`) on the M5 Max, plus a Qwen3-8B compression with astcenc and its KLD against BF16, before and after a LoRA recovery step.
- **Kill criterion.** Texture bandwidth below 90% of buffer bandwidth, or KLD worse than MLX 4-bit at equal bytes after recovery ([Part XII](part12-quantization.md) §11).
- **Chapters.** [Part XII](part12-quantization.md) §11; [Part XXVI](part26-roadmap-4-years.md) §3.1.

### Bet G. Drafting on the Apple Neural Engine (Part XXVI B8)

- **Why it is transformative.** Drafting on the ANE in parallel with GPU verification would take the draft cost c off the GPU's critical path. Apple's Mirror-SD reports 2.8–5.8× wall time, about 30% over EAGLE-3, losslessly (R8 §2.6).
- **What must be true.**
  - Mirror-SD's result transfers to one chip. It ran on 8 M2 Ultra GPUs in tensor parallel plus 8 Neural Engines (Part XXVI §3.1); on one M5 Max the GPU and the ANE share the 614 GB/s bus ([Part XXI](part21-speculative.md) §8), so the verify pass must not slow down much under concurrent ANE traffic.
  - A public API (Core ML or Core AI) runs a drafter with a short enough step. Private APIs are excluded by the private-API lint (XXVII-T6).
  - The ANE draft step beats Sky's GPU draft step, whose target is c ≤ 0.035 for a 0.6B draft against a 32B target (XXI-T6).
- **First experiment.** Run the 32B target's decode on the GPU while a 0.6B drafter runs on the ANE through Core AI or Core ML. Measure the draft step's time on the ANE and the GPU verify slowdown under the concurrent traffic.
- **Kill criterion.** Effective throughput not above the GPU drafter's (Part XXVI B8). It is a [MOONSHOT], year 3 at the earliest.
- **Chapters.** [Part XXI](part21-speculative.md) §8; [Part XXVI](part26-roadmap-4-years.md) §3.1, §6; [Part XXVII](part27-moat.md) §2.1.

### Bet H. `portable`: the same bits on every Apple GPU (Part XXVI B3)

- **Why it is transformative.** Scientific Sky promises the same bits everywhere. Extending that promise to LLM inference would make outputs reproducible across Macs, for evaluation, audited deployments and research, and would restore the spec's guarantee that "tuning changes speed, never results" for tensor programs ([Part VI](part06-architecture.md) §8.8). No competitor offers it: mlx-lm's temperature-0 output differed between an M3 Ultra and an M5 Max (mlx-lm#1280; [Part V](part05-competitors.md) §4.13).
- **What must be true.**
  - Only order-preserving tuning parameters are allowed: no MPP on the decode path, or fixed tiles where MPP is required ([Part X](part10-autotuner.md) §3.1).
  - Sky owns its transcendental functions (`exp`, `rsqrt`) in MSL, because the operating system's Metal compiler and math library supply them today ([Part III](part03-current-performance.md) §11, the GPU analogue of F10).
  - The speed cost is acceptable to the owner as a product mode. It has not been measured.
- **First experiment.** Compare softmax and SiLU outputs bit for bit across two macOS versions and two GPU families (Part III §11). Then measure decode, verify and prefill with order-changing parameters fixed at canonical values against `inference`'s tuned values ([Part X](part10-autotuner.md) §14).
- **Kill criterion.** A cost above what the owner accepts for a product mode; `portable` then stays a test mode (Part XXVI B3; Part X §14). Study in year 2, product in year 3.
- **Chapters.** [Part VI](part06-architecture.md) §8.8; [Part X](part10-autotuner.md) §3.1, §14; [Part III](part03-current-performance.md) §11; [Part XXVI](part26-roadmap-4-years.md) §3.1; [Part XXVII](part27-moat.md) §3.3.

### Bet I. A self-optimizing per-chip plan database

- **Why it is transformative.** MLX ships tunings within days of each chip's launch, from hardware Sky does not have ([Part XXVII](part27-moat.md) §2.1). A loop that notices when measured steps diverge from the performance model (a residual above 15% for 3 sampled steps, an OS update, a new chip or model), re-tunes under verification, and seeds from neighboring chips could keep plans current on machines Sky's team never touched ([Part X](part10-autotuner.md) §8, §11). An opt-in exchange of tuning records would extend it across users (§10).
- **What must be true.**
  - Sampling costs nothing visible: one extra timestamp per kernel on 1 step in 1,000, about 386 timestamps for Qwen3-32B.
  - Transfer works within a family: seeded M5 Pro tuning from M5 Max entries within 3% of a full search at ≤ 20% of its time (X-T10, G8, which needs a second M5-family chip).
  - Timing is honest enough that three tunings of one instance agree within 3% (Part X §14).
  - The owner settles hosting, signing, consent and legal review before any exchange code is written ([Part XXVI](part26-roadmap-4-years.md) §3.2). The loop proposes and never writes the database without the user's action.
- **First experiment.** X-T10's transfer test on the M5 family, and a count of how often stored order-changing entries change bits or fail tolerance after an OS update (Part X §14).
- **Kill criterion.** G5: if the autotuner improves hot kernels by < 5% over hand-picked defaults on small-M prefill and verify shapes, the general layers freeze (R-13), and this bet with them. If decode-GEMV tuning gains < 1%, `-O3` skips those kernels (Part X §14). Part XXVII §3.1 rates tuning data weak against Apple, so the bet's value is robustness for users, not a moat.
- **Chapters.** [Part X](part10-autotuner.md) §8–§11, §14; [Part XXVI](part26-roadmap-4-years.md) §3.2, §5; [Part XXVII](part27-moat.md) §3.1; [Part XXVIII](part28-risks.md) R-13, R-14.

### Bet J. Hybrid Gated-DeltaNet + MTP models with exact rollback

- **Why it is transformative.** The biggest physics lever in 2026 is the model (R8 §9). Qwen3.6/3.8-27B streams 14.4 GB per token, 20% less than Qwen3-32B, with a 42.6 tok/s roofline on the M5 Max, 4× less KV and a trained MTP head; DSpark drafters reach τ ≈ 4.4–5.7 on an H100 (R8 §1.7, §2.3). A buyer evaluating "Qwen 32B-class on M5 Max" will run this model ([Part XXVIII](part28-risks.md) R-9). Exact speculative rollback of recurrent state is something nobody ships ([Part XXVII](part27-moat.md) §3.2, P2), and mlx-lm cannot speculate on these models at all (#1446).
- **What must be true.**
  - The Gated DeltaNet recurrence runs per position inside the verify pass, in the same floating-point order as plain decode. The chunked parallel scan is rejected for verification because its order differs; rollback uses a double buffer plus replay (2 × 151 MB of state) rather than per-position checkpoints ([Part XXI](part21-speculative.md) §7.3; [Part XIV](part14-kv-cache.md) XIV-T6).
  - Commit costs ≤ 3% of a round (XXI-T13).
  - MTP acceptance holds at Qwen's recommended sampling settings.
  - We know the bar: on an M5 Max, MTPLX self-reports 65.2 tok/s at Qwen's sampling settings and 87.6 "optimized" on Qwen3.8-27B, and 30.37 → 81.74 tok/s on Qwen3.6-27B (UNVERIFIED); an independent M3 Max test measured +24–28% (R8 §2.7; [Part XXI](part21-speculative.md) §11).
- **First experiment.** Reproduce MTPLX's claim on our M5 Max under the Part XXIII protocol (V-T11, in [issues.md](issues.md) I-04). In parallel, VII-T9: the `deltanet.scan` composite against transformers' `qwen3_next` layer on the reference interpreter, and VII-M6: checkpoints against replay, in time and bytes per verify step. Part XXV starts this lane at week 20, so that G5's reproduction can move the headline in time (§16.1).
- **Kill criterion.** G5, PIVOT: if MTPLX's ≥ 60 tok/s reproduces, Sky's speculative headline moves to this model and must beat that number exactly. G7, CLAIM: if Sky cannot run the hybrids with exact rollback by G7, the speculative headline may not be presented as state of the art (R-9).
- **Chapters.** [Part XXI](part21-speculative.md) §7.3, §14, XXI-T13; [Part XIV](part14-kv-cache.md) §8, XIV-T6; [Part VII](part07-tensor-ir.md) §5.13.7, VII-T9; [Part XXV](part25-roadmap-12-months.md) §16.1; [Part XXVIII](part28-risks.md) R-9.
